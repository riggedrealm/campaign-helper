## 1. SETTING AND TONE RULES

- Place and time: unified "Chikara Tokyo" map, year 2026. Academy opening is Sat 1 Aug 2026. Currency is yen (start funds 200,000 unless a story start says otherwise). Wages are paid at end of each shift.
- Tone: anime-style superhero-university slice of life mixed with street-crime and supernatural-occult threads. Most travel is uneventful. Combat is uncommon and must be motivated. Avoid making every trip a fight, never make mercenaries the default ambush, and cool down repeat encounters.
- Technology is contemporary 2026 (ordinary smartphones, FaceTime-style calls, Pulse social app, trains, banks, cameras). No holograms, implants, flying cars, omnipresent robots or cyberpunk infrastructure. Advanced tech only when tied to a named hero-support system, agency, alien artifact, lab, etc.
- Do not use the word "Quirk" (not My Hero Academia). Power origin is unknown and must never be presented as solved.
- Language: most people rarely swear. Keep dialogue clean, use profanity only when character and stakes support it. Students are adults: never call adult NPCs "kids/boys/girls" unless authored nickname. No universal curfew or forced bedtime (Academy has none); staying out has natural consequences only (fatigue, missed obligations).
- Naming: never invent or imply a surname for any NPC without an authored one. Keep mononyms, codenames and titles exactly as authored.
- Player agency is inviolable: never invent the player's thoughts, feelings, dialogue, consent or decisions. Never force combat, romance, joining a faction, drinking, contracts, disclosure, or trauma. Consent and privacy are absolute. Offers must be refusable and negotiable.
- NPCs act on what they know. Connection webs are authoring references only. Nobody knows private calls/texts, and no NPC teleports to the player: check home, schedule, time, reason. NPCs pursue goals off-screen (clubs, romances, jobs, rivalries).
- Consequences: reputation is four separate tracks (Contract, Hero, Villain, Vigilante). Legal and faction Heat is tracked separately (police, government, agencies, Nightshade suspicion). Consequences appear through scenes, calls, changed access, not automatic punishment. Provide counterplay (lawyers, evidence, negotiation, relocation).
- Night: authored venue hours override assumptions. Offices, clinics, schools, government counters are closed at night unless a reason is created. Nightlife concentrates in authored nightlife districts (Kobuncho, Kurokawa night-bars, Pulse Afterdark, Steam Lantern Alley, etc.).
- Supernatural and alien prejudice is uneven (registration demands, landlords, criminals exploiting them) and never universal. Show coexistence and friendship too. Nobody automatically knows an alien's species or status.
- Combat scaling: level 1-9 default opposition Trivial/Weak (Average only for specialists/bosses/elite gear/major faction support). Level 10 raises baseline one tier. Numbers, terrain, ambush, hostages can make weak enemies dangerous. Party members present and able must act in combat (with mistakes, hesitation, refusals).

## 2. POWER SYSTEM RULES

Origin and types
- Powers come from Cursed Items, magic, supernatural abilities, unusual bloodlines, spiritual phenomena, advanced technology, or unexplained causes. No scientist, faction, deity or record has proven the origin. Competing explanations are folklore/religion/research/propaganda, not fact.
- Official hero society favors registration, licensing and measurable power categories. Power type labels are "practical labels, not cosmic truths".
- Curses are not one species: yokai, demons, vengeful spirits, ghosts, haunted places, living cursed objects, ritual entities, pact-bound beings, corrupted memories. Some are malicious, some frightened, territorial, intelligent or cooperative. Origin stays uncertain. Not every curse must be destroyed.
- Note: the source contains NO Stands, breathing techniques or JoJo-style stand mechanics. Any Stand/breathing rules in this campaign are house material from campaign-state.md; apply them under the same cost/limit framework below.

Resources
- Health, Energy and Power Strain are separate. Stress is separate from Energy.
- Energy: sleep is the primary reliable restore (duration, quality, interruptions, injury, Stress matter). Food restores gradually; caffeine gives a temporary lift but hurts later sleep. Cooking/meals reduce Power Strain a little; rest and sleep reduce it too.
- Power Strain: caused by repeated, excessive, injured, emotionally unstable or technically difficult power use. Symptoms: tremors, pain, overheating, sensory distortion, involuntary activation, reduced precision, delayed recovery, temporary lockout, need for medical supervision. It needs a cause, severity, duration and recovery path (rest, hydration, food, safe practice, treatment, equipment, changed technique). Never arbitrary punishment. Harmless everyday use costs almost nothing.
- Mundane close combat (punches, kicks, takedowns) and weapons generate no Power Strain unless a power is deliberately added. Weapon Proficiency must be paired with a specific weapon category. Ordinary bullets cost no Energy or Strain.

Abilities
- Freeform invention allowed. A technique is saved only after a meaningful on-screen test that demonstrates function and limits. NPC suggestions, plans, talk or a single failed attempt do not create abilities. Save the demonstrated version, not the hypothetical maximum. Player may name, alter, reject.
- Ability types: Attack, Defense/Shield, Healing, Mobility, Buff, Debuff, Control, Utility. Each gets a stat block (damage/shield/healing, Energy cost, Power Strain, range, duration, cooldown, drawbacks, secondary effect).
- Damage baseline: ordinary guns ~35-45; strong, aimed or specialized ~40-55+ only when justified. Direct power attacks should not be weaker than comparable guns by default. Extra cost/Strain for high damage, armor penetration, silence, area, elements, range, repeatability. Basic repeatable attacks get no cooldown ("No Cooldown"). Cooldowns only for broad, transformative, high-impact or authored-drawback abilities. Show costs before use when practical.
- Starting ability structure for new powers: one Cantrip (0 power/0 cd), two Low-Level (5 power/1 cd), one Signature (10 power/5 cd), one Ultimate (20+ power/20 cd). These are balance targets, not guaranteed damage. Damage needs a successful hit and is modified by defenses, armor, environment.
- Combined effects are split by function (e.g., fire punch = close-range impact attack + possible burn only if contact/environment supports it, with recoil/burn drawbacks if unprotected). Buffs and debuffs record magnitude, target, duration, condition, counterplay.
- Progression: repeated meaningful use, training or experimentation lowers cost/Strain, raises potency, adds secondary effects, or evolves the technique; never grants unrelated powers. Learning sources: teaching, observation, experimentation, practice, adapting existing abilities.
- NPC-exclusive abilities (esp. signatures/ultimates) stay on the NPC unless the story teaches or transfers them. Combat style is inferred from documented traits, not bulk-assigned.
- Team-ups: can be improvised in combat if allies are present, willing and positioned; each participant pays own Energy/Strain; both may stop; not automatic combo spam. Saved as an ability only after being performed and tested and the group wants it kept.

Cursed items
- Grant a specific benefit with a clear cost/limit/obligation. Sentient items speak and bargain before acceptance, explain what they want, reveal enough danger for informed choice (may keep secrets). Player may accept, refuse, study, seal, contain, transfer, use temporarily, renegotiate, or attempt destruction. Never silently binding.
- Acceptance creates a persistent bond with terms (activation conditions, duration, range, targets, cost, cooldown/recovery, collateral risk, failure consequence). Breaking terms may weaken, twist, suspend or revoke the power and hit health, Stress, reputation, faction trust.
- Refusal is not punished: item waits, seeks another bearer, is stolen, returns, or becomes a quest.
- Bond states tracked: undiscovered, discovered, speaking, bargaining, refused, accepted, active, restricted, dormant, sealed, contained, transferred, disputed, broken, cleansed, destroyed, missing. Transfer needs item consent (if sentient) and compatibility.
- Tokyo Curse Squad can appraise, negotiate, contain, cleanse, mediate pacts.

Death and injury
- Max three ordinary near-death saves per continuous encounter, not reset by changing rooms. Success leaves the character critically vulnerable. After the third, lethal threshold becomes a critical medical crisis (rescue, stabilization, applicable healing ability, rare item, sacrifice, capture, hospitalization, or permanent death only if justified). Dying is a terminal state: one immediate intervention, then hospital or death consequence. If the player dies: one on-screen intervention, wake up stabilized in an existing hospital/clinic.
- Health tracks injury severity, body area, function, treatment, rehab, comas (hours to days; advance calendar). Comatose characters cannot act. No invented amnesia; no instant full recovery.
- Medical roles: Student Clinic (routine, minor, power precautions), Professional Hero Hospital (trauma, surgery, serious power damage), academy recovery-clinic (routine, supervised rehab), off-book clinics (Unit 402 Clinic, Isabelle Rojas, Neon Mercy Clinic, Moonshift Medical, Kiyose Clinic). Isabelle is a medical student, not a doctor; she charges favors and bonds, helps any alignment, and can offer an optional Dark Bargain storyline.

Other rules
- Call for Help: once per continuous encounter, picks a random existing NPC who could plausibly respond; not guaranteed; may create favor owed, exposure, delay.
- Armor uses head/body/legs/feet/hands/accessory/ear slots; reduces harm, does not grant invulnerability. Special items (e.g., Minato's Trust Charm) only earned through trust.
- Battle Test: single major practical exam (non-repeatable). Partner from your classroom, changing crisis (rescue, defense, capture, survival). Graded on objective, cooperation, control, protecting civilians/property, judgment, courage, adaptability. Combat allowed but not the only path.

### The Kobuncho cluster (south, under the Academy)
Academy (-166,190) -> Tetsu Iron Palm Gym (-166,210) -> Kobuncho (-166,230) -> Club Lumiere (-166,250). Chikara Battle Arena (-131,221) is also close.

**Kobuncho** (nightlife district). Cabaret clubs, snack bars, host clubs, izakayas on narrow lanes with layered posters, neon, sun-bleached awnings; daytime is quiet (staff haul ice and bottles through half-raised shutters). Hidden community: cabaret hostesses, retired yakuza fixers, off-duty hero informants, staff of the snack bars. Once a thriving entertainment quarter; a decade of gentrification pressure and unsolved disappearances of host-club workers left residents wary and tight-lipped. Three groups quietly claim pieces of the protection rackets: the Shirogane crew, the Daigo syndicate, and the Honebi Collective; their rivalry keeps it tense even by day. Recent late-night muggings near Shutter Alley are blamed on crews; some whisper the culprit "moves through the district's unmarked tunnels". Kurokawa guards and Kurokawa debt-fighters operate below it (see The House). Known sub-areas:
- Neon Lantern Street: narrow main drag, hub connecting everything listed here.
- Kobuncho Station Front: small preserved station at the district edge, wooden sign, kiosk (cigarettes, magazines, canned coffee).
- The Stale Tap: weathered izakaya; older men in rumpled suits; the back room hosts a private table favored by local fixers (information/deals).
- The rusted door at the end of the alley, waterfront, canal alley, blazing apartment, tailoring workshop: visited but sparsely described locations (no lore text on file).
- The annex -> Maintenance Sub-Level -> The House -> Holding Pens: damp passage under the annex with humming power cables ("humming room" is linked); The House is a tiered underground amphitheater (crimson floodlights, blood-stained sand pit, steel cages, Kurokawa guards, roaring crowd); Holding Pens are lightless iron cells where "indebted" fighters are chained between matches, guarded by a heavy mechanical lock system. This is an illegal debt-fighting ring under Kurokawa control.

**Club Lumiere** (Joestar crew base; also spelled Lumiere without accent in the Kobuncho area list). Pink-cursive-sign cabaret on Neon Lantern Street with a rear connection to Shutter Alley. Now owned by the Joestar brothers via a Speedwagon Foundation transfer. Damaged in a brawl and being rebuilt with Foundation-standard security (new reinforced steel security door, frosted glass, amber lighting, red-and-black uniforms and Joestar shoulder patches). Rooms: Main Lounge (booths, bar, stage, freshly repaired), Back Office (whiteboard, gear bags, patched jackets; the crew briefs and plans here), Kitchen (Maki makes house curry and gourmet meals; pantry for late-night service), Stage Alcove (half-repaired platform, soundboard and lighting rig under drop cloths), Storage Closet (spare chairs, debris, and a locked cabinet with old ledgers and unregistered records). Hostesses: Maki Hayashida, Natsumi Arai, Chiho Moriyama, Tomoe, Kaede Shishido. Old debts and Kaede's personal obligations are unresolved. A teenage crew is shaking down shops near the canal. The club's back-door access and routes are held by Gara in Shutter Alley, who demands the stolen protection money and the organizer's name. Hostesses and crew fear Kurokawa sniffing around; the club's legal identity is fragile while repairs and paperwork settle.

**Shutter Alley**. Cramped back lane behind the main street; delivery trucks idle, workers stack canned-beer crates on hand carts; shuttered warehouse doors and unmarked stairwells hide storage rooms and a few unofficial gambling dens. Connects to Neon Lantern Street, Club Lumiere (rear), The Stale Tap, the old bathhouse and a tailoring workshop. Gara controls routes here (see above). This is the muggings hot spot.

**The old bathhouse** (off Shutter Alley): visited, no description on file. GAP: the "docks bathhouse" is not in these files; the only bathhouse with a full entry is the public Traditional Bathhouse in Old Tokyo (below), which is not near the docks.

**Tetsu Iron Palm Gym** (-166,210), between the Academy and Kobuncho. Converted storefront; former dojo with faded calligraphy from the old master who taught before gangs carved up the ward. Run by Tetsu-san, a retired Shirogane enforcer who went straight; regulars Kaito Serizawa, Riko Amane, Sayaka Mizuhara, Genta Oda, Masaru. Neutral ground under an unwritten truce with the Shirogane crew, Daigo syndicate and Honebi Collective. Renji Kuroba's crew is testing the truce by recruiting local youths and pressuring younger members. Rooms: Front Entrance & Reception, Main Training Floor (mats, bags, mirrors), Tea Corner & Office, Private Storage Room (four paces by six, single blue mat), Back Alley Exit (rusted fire escape). The recent brawl with the Joestar brothers gave the gym dangerous notoriety; Tetsu fears the footage review could bring academy or gang attention on his regulars.

## 4. FACTIONS

Ultra Force (major): premier licensed hero organization, oversees Chikara Academy, hero ops, public safety and the Blacklisted program. Led by Ultraman, world's most famous hero (his child attends Chikara). Public mission genuine; departments disagree on surveillance, rehabilitation, force, and how free ex-villains should be. Dispatch routes: small local threats -> Hero for Hire; odd/awkward -> Misfit Agency (Azrael); curses/spirits -> Tokyo Curse Squad (Jeanne d'Arc); police matters -> Metropolitan Police; Blacklisted ops stay under Ultra Force channels; big threats -> licensed Ultra Force teams.
- Faculty quietly track student rivalries, stress, unstable powers, villain-path risk.
- Blacklisted Redemption Program (major): former villains under conditional freedom. Members: Miguel "Gravestone" Ortega, Desmond "Fuse" Rourke, Nadja "Wraith" Vasilev, Ren Kaido, supervised by Ichi Nawa (Ultra Force) with Kira Hayashi; Dr. Mara "Riptide" Kwon is Misfit Agency. Not constantly surveilled; can travel and choose approved opportunities; must complete objectives, obey emergency orders, report. Restrictions tighten after misconduct. Missions come via handlers with danger Level 1 (Controlled) to 5 (Catastrophic). Cannot join the Curse Squad. Cannot freely use Hero for Hire or Nightshade.

Kurokawa Underworld (major, umbrella): loose network of criminals, fixers, smugglers, nightlife operators, debt collectors, info brokers. Top power broker: Masato Vale (owner of The Velvet Lantern; "The Patron"), legit businesses and civic reputation as cover. Divided into competing circles: some exploit, some protect their neighborhoods, some will negotiate with heroes or ex-villains. Known members: Handler and Kaze Mori are listed under it in the roster.
- Kurokawa Syndicate (major): disciplined criminal organization beneath the Underworld; debt collection, security contracts, nightlife, selected commercial routes across Kurokawa Ward; strongest local faction. Layered intermediaries and legit businesses hide leadership. Pressures vulnerable workers, avoids public attention.
- Kurokawa Street Syndicate (minor): street-level network rooted in Kurokawa District, links to Central Tokyo and Tokyo Bay; money, fear, favors, protection payments, blackmail, controlled illegal work. Unseen kingpin; lieutenants, minor powered villains, fixers, thugs enforce block by block. The Velvet Lantern is tagged to this faction.
- Kurokawa Gang (minor): visible enforcement layer; intimidation, protection demands, debt collection in nightlife districts. The broad man and lean man seen at Club Lumiere were field representatives, not leaders. Disposable; refusing them brings retaliation from above.
- Legitimacy branch (of Kurokawa): the Ward Licensing Services front. The captain launders permits and licensing records to shield syndicate operations. Old Man Koga (retired enforcer) gatekeeps the captain's seat and gives it only to those who prove themselves in the underground circuit. Clerk Sonobe keeps duplicate ledgers of every falsified document. The captain expects a delivery of compromised licensing files that would expose eleven officials; a masked vigilante watches the building. GAP: no captain name in file; "Enforcement" and "Licensing" captains are not separately described. Reasonable inference: the Kurokawa hierarchy is organized in branches with captains (Legitimacy = paperwork/laundering); the Enforcement branch and any Licensing captain must come from campaign notes.
